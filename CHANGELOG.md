## [1.0.0] - 2026-11-10

The first version of On Call Calendar: duty rosters, on-call rotations and if-no-answer routing for MikoPBX 2026.6.34 or later.

Added:

- **Schedules with their own internal numbers.** Each schedule's number is registered in MikoPBX as a module extension and appears in the destination lists of incoming routes, off-work time rules, IVR menus and forwarding as "Module: <name> (<number>)".
- **The if-no-answer chain in the dialplan.** A schedule's number rings the people on duty through MikoPBX's normal extension dialplan (call history, recording, every registered device, busy handling) with their personal forwarding turned off for that call, including for a caller transferred to the schedule's number (a caller they transfer on gets the transfer target's own forwarding and voicemail again), then an optional backup person or queue, then a final destination (voicemail by default). Outside covered hours calls go to a separate destination. Phones can transfer callers to a schedule's number.
- **Who is on duty is read from Asterisk's database** when a call arrives, so plan changes never reload the dialplan.
- **Rosters, rotations and overrides.** A roster assigns people to named shifts on weekdays and repeats over 1–4 weeks; a rotation hands duty from one person to the next every few days, optionally only within daily active hours; an override (cover, swap, take over, send elsewhere) beats both for an exact time range. Where a roster's shifts overlap, the one lower in its list wins; where a roster and a rotation overlap, the one higher in the When Plans Overlap list wins. Covered time with nobody to ring is a gap and goes to the backup. Shifts follow wall-clock time in the PBX's time zone, overnight shifts belong to the day they start, and daylight-saving changes make a shift an hour shorter or longer.
- **A worker keeps the answer published**: at start, every minute, right when an answer changes, and immediately after a change on the module page. Each answer expires after at most three minutes, so if the worker stops, calls ask a live lookup (an AGI script) instead; if that fails too, the call goes to the backup and the final destination.
- **The module page** (Modules → On Call Calendar):
  - **Home:** the list of schedules, with New Schedule beside its heading and the PBX's clock; one card per schedule with who is on duty now and until when, what comes next, uncovered hours in the next 14 days, how many places route to it, and Cover Now. Schedules that need attention (a gap now, gaps coming, nothing routing to them) come first; disabled ones last.
  - **New schedule wizard** in numbered steps, ending in a Create / Cancel bar: pick a template (Shift Duty, On-Call or Blank), a name and a free number, tick the incoming routes it should answer, the on-call rotation if any, and the if-no-answer chain.
  - **Calendar** (FullCalendar 7 with its Pulse theme in the admin cabinet's colours; month, week and list views) in the PBX's own time zone: planned shifts in each person's colour, overrides solid, gaps striped. Click a shift to cover it, swap it or send its calls elsewhere; drag across empty time to add an override; drag or resize overrides to move them. Phones open the list view.
  - **Roster grid:** shifts as rows, weekdays as columns, one grid per week of the cycle; click a cell (or focus it and press Enter) to choose who works it. Empty cells are marked as going to the backup. Shifts can be added, edited, deleted and reordered.
  - **A schedule** opens with a back button and its name and number above the tabs Calendar, Roster, Rotation and Activity, with Settings on the right; the tab bar scrolls sideways on narrow screens.
  - **Rotation:** people in turn order, first handover, turn length, active-hours windows, an end date, and the current turn followed by the next handovers.
  - **Settings**, in sections: name, and on/off with a warning that calls to the number fail while it's off; how calls are answered, as numbered steps (ring the people on duty, the backup, the final destination) plus the outside-hours destination; what callers and phones see (greeting, caller name prefix); a test call; linked incoming routes (link, unlink, a warning for routes whose timeout is shorter than the chain) and everything else that routes to the number; the backup per roster and rotation; which plan wins when they overlap; and deleting the schedule.
  - **Activity:** the activity report (a period preset or dates, figures as cards, a table per person) and the change log: who changed what and when, each change in the page's own words (field labels, people's and routes' names, before and after).
  - The page follows the admin cabinet's Semantic UI: tabbed panels, section headings with their actions on the right, quiet tables, Semantic status labels, and deleting asks in a short confirmation named after the action.
  - **Interface text** follows one style: Title Case for headings, tabs, buttons, options, field labels and column headers, sentence case for hints, statuses and messages. Every error says what to do next, confirmations say what happened, empty tabs explain what belongs there, and buttons with only an icon have a name for screen readers. People are shown with their initials in their schedule colour, and every screen works down to phone width.
- **Backup per roster and rotation:** a roster or rotation can have its own backup (a person, a queue, or none) that replaces the schedule's while it decides who is on duty, so one number can have, say, a daytime roster falling back to the reception queue and a night rotation with no backup. Linked routes' timeouts take the longest backup into account.
- **Activity report:** per schedule and period (up to three months): calls answered on duty, by the backup or others, by voicemail and unanswered, the median time to answer, and per person hours on duty and calls answered, with a CSV export of the calls (values a spreadsheet could read as a formula are kept as text). When a call reaches a schedule the dialplan leaves a note in AstDB (no PHP during the call); once Asterisk has no channel of the call left, the worker reads its call records and stores how it ended. Calls are kept for 400 days.
- **\*22 take-over from a phone:** a member dials \*22 (or \*22 and the schedule's number, if they belong to several) to be on duty from now until the plan next changes, at most a week. It is stored as a "take over" override, audited, and shown on the calendar. The caller is identified by the PJSIP endpoint the call came from.
- **Caller name prefix:** an optional prefix (for example SERVICE) put before the caller's name on the phones a schedule rings, as MikoPBX queues do, so whoever answers knows which line it is.
- **Greeting:** an optional sound file played to the caller before anyone is rung, as early media, so the call is not answered (or charged) until a person picks up; ringing is then played to the caller.
- **Test Call** under Settings: rings a chosen phone first and, once answered, calls the schedule through its real chain.
- **Rights for access groups:** View, Edit Duty and Edit Settings, granted through MikoPBX's access control (MIKO's Access control management module) as the page's actions; the README lists which to tick. Every request is checked against the role's rights, change requests need a token that carries them, and the page shows only the controls a person may use. Rights apply to every schedule; only Edit Settings sees the PBX's incoming routes, and employees' mobile numbers are never sent to the page.
- **Linking incoming routes** from the wizard or the settings: the route's destination becomes the schedule's number and its timeout is raised to fit the chain; unlinking puts both back, unless the route was changed by hand since. Deleting a schedule unlinks its routes first and is refused while anything else still routes to its number.
- **Uninstalling cleans up after itself:** the AGI links, the module's Asterisk database keys and its sidebar entry are removed. Without kept settings, the incoming routes the module linked are put back as they were and the schedules' numbers are released, except a number something else still points at.
- **Every change is audited** with who made it, and validated on the server whatever the page sends. A schedule holds up to 20 shifts, 20 rosters and rotations, 21 active-hours windows per rotation and 500 overrides still to come, so no plan can make working out who is on duty slow for the others.
- **English and Russian.**
- **For contributors:** a real-call probe (`tools/callsim`) with 21 scenarios run through a test PBX with softphones and a fake carrier; CI on every push (unit tests, lint, a banned-code check, PHPStan against the MikoPBX core, npm audit, and a check that the committed bundles match their sources), a tag-triggered Release workflow that builds the install zip into a draft release, CONTRIBUTING.md and SECURITY.md.
